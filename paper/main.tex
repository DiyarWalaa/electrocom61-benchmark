\begin{abstract}
ElectroCom61 is a public object detection dataset for recovering reusable
components from discarded electronic equipment, containing 2{,}121 images of 61
component classes carrying 12{,}937 annotations. Its published
train/validation/test partition cannot evaluate 15 of those 61 classes: they
hold no instances in either the validation or the test set. They are not the
rare classes. Eight of the ten most
heavily annotated classes are among them, and what decides whether a class is
evaluable is which capture session photographed it. We audit the dataset from
its released files alone, release a corrected partition as a manifest that holds
the published split sizes of 1478/438/205 images, and train five detectors on
both partitions under one configuration, with a single documented deviation: the
transformer detector needs a learning rate a hundredfold lower to train at all.
Correcting the partition raises every model's score on both accuracy metrics ---
mean average precision at an intersection-over-union threshold of 0.5 (mAP@50)
and averaged over ten thresholds to 0.95 (mAP@50--95) --- on both evaluation
partitions and without exception. The defect had been understating reported
performance rather than inflating it, the reverse of what a defective partition
is normally taken to do. The detector recording the highest mAP@50 leads on
neither mAP@50--95 nor inference latency and is not the one this application
should deploy, a distinction no prior study on this dataset could have drawn:
none of the three reports a timing measurement. The corrected manifest, the
training configurations and the run records behind every figure are released.
\end{abstract}
