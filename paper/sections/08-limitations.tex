Every accuracy figure in this paper comes from a single training run. There is
no variance estimate for any of them, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:metric-choice} reports the three middle-ranked architectures as
a group rather than an ordering: differences of 0.0001 to 0.0023 mAP@50--95
cannot be separated from run-to-run variation that was never measured. The
latency figures are better off, because each architecture was timed twice and
the two measurements disagree by at most 0.24~ms
(Section~\ref{sec:efficiency-measurement}); the accuracy measurements have no
equivalent number, and none should be inferred from the latency one.

Every finding here is measured on one dataset. The mechanism behind the coverage
defect is not specific to ElectroCom61 and should be expected wherever data is
collected in sessions and split by image (Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}), but
that is an argument about the mechanism, not a demonstration. Nothing in this
paper shows the defect on a second dataset, and the frequency with which it
occurs in published benchmarks is unknown.

No difference reported here is tested for statistical significance. Magnitudes
are given, and where a repeatability figure exists it is given beside them, but
no confidence interval, hypothesis test or effect-size estimate appears anywhere
in this work. Comparative statements should be read as descriptions of what was
measured on these runs, not as claims that the differences would survive
replication.

The contamination figures are floors rather than estimates, and three separate
restrictions make them so. Only pairs whose class inventories match exactly are
ever compared, so a genuine duplicate with one component occluded is never
scored (Section~\ref{sec:duplicate-contamination}). Pairs carrying two annotated
boxes or fewer are excluded from the ranking, which is the conservative choice
for a ranking and the permissive one for a count of zero
(Section~\ref{sec:correction-limits}). And the corrected split's figure at the
loosest threshold is not independent of the grouping that produced it, because
that grouping used the same threshold (Section~\ref{sec:grouping-rule}); the
figures at the two stricter thresholds carry no such dependence.

The fifteen rescued classes hold between five and ten test instances each. Five
instances is the threshold at which a class becomes measurable, not a sample
size from which a per-class average precision can be quoted with confidence. The
corrected split makes those classes evaluable and claims nothing further about
how well they are evaluated.

The grouping threshold and the allocation that follows from it are established
for one seed. The allocator is greedy, so a different seed explores a different
region of the search space and could hold the frozen split sizes where this one
fails or fail where this one holds. The sweep in
Section~\ref{sec:tau-choice} answers the question for seed 20260804 and does not
establish that 15 seconds is the right threshold in general.

The confidence threshold used at evaluation is fixed by the Ultralytics release
rather than stated explicitly in the configuration
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}). That is this study's own instance of the
reporting gap it documents in prior work: an evaluation protocol can be recorded
in full and still contain a value determined by a library version. Stating the
library version is what makes it recoverable, which is the reason
Section~\ref{sec:training-configuration} treats that version as part of the
specification, but it is a mitigation and not a fix.

Latency was measured on a single Tesla P100, chosen because prior work on this
dataset reports the same device and holding it constant keeps the comparison
against those studies intact. It is not the hardware the deployment argument in
Section~\ref{sec:deployment} concerns: a recycling facility would install an
embedded accelerator or an industrial edge device, where memory bandwidth,
kernel support and operator coverage all differ from a datacentre GPU. A
benchmark of nine YOLO variants across three edge systems-on-chip reports memory
bandwidth governing robustness in that setting \citep{kong2026edge}. Relative
orderings between architectures need not transfer, and the architectures most
likely to move are the ones whose advantage here is in postprocessing rather
than convolution. Nothing in this paper measures that.

Every statement this paper makes about prior work is read from published PDFs,
which are not part of the accompanying repository. The scripts that verify every
other number in this paper cannot check any of them, and that includes the
transcribed parameter counts underlying the complexity comparison in
Section~\ref{sec:disagreement} --- the one strong claim here whose input is a
hand transcription rather than a measurement. Those values are recorded in a
committed file with the table each came from, so they can be re-checked against
their sources, but re-checking them is a manual step that this study's tooling
does not perform.

Every YOLO run consumed its full hundred-epoch budget without triggering early
stopping, so none of them had plateaued when training ended. The accuracy
figures reported for them are therefore a lower bound at this budget rather than
converged results, and the ordering between architectures could change under a
longer one. RT-DETR-l is the exception in both directions: it did stop early, at
epochs 59 and 73 of its two runs, so it alone is reported at a point its own
early-stopping criterion selected --- which means the comparison sets four
unconverged models against one converged one. The budget was copied from prior work
(Section~\ref{sec:training-configuration}) precisely so that it could not be
offered as an explanation for a difference between this study and those; holding
it fixed is what makes the comparison controlled and also what leaves this
limitation in place.
