   answer to the fused/unfused question is: NOT STATED, in both papers.

5. AN INTERNAL INCONSISTENCY, present in both papers, between body text and table. Both
   papers' Section IV states "Despite having only ~2.5M parameters, YOLOv10s achieves an
   mAP@0.5 of 86.7%". Both papers' TABLE I gives YOLOv10s as 2.6 and YOLOv11s as 2.5. The
   ~2.5M attribution in the prose belongs to the adjacent row. The tables are internally
   consistent with each other and are the values transcribed above; the prose is not
   consistent with the tables.

6. [ELHENIDY] prints one further complexity statement, in Section V "Conclusion and future
   work": YOLOv12s "achieves the highest mAP@0.5 (89.1%) and recall (68.5%) while
   maintaining a moderate parameter count (19.7M)". This agrees with TABLE I.
   [GAIB] prints the equivalent for its own model in Section IV: YOLOv13s "With 648 layers
   and 2.5M parameters". This agrees with its TABLE I.

7. [ELHENIDY] Section IV contains the phrase "while maintaining a compact model size". No
   number accompanies it. It is a qualitative statement, not a model-size figure.

IMPACT ON THE MANUSCRIPT

No change required to the three figures. 2.5 M for YOLO11s, 19.7 M for YOLOv12s and 60.8 M
for YOLOv9s are all confirmed in both papers' tables, and data/published_complexity.csv
records them correctly for both sources.

Two things are worth carrying into the prose, because both bear on how much weight a
published parameter count can take:

  - Neither paper states whether its counts are fused or unfused. If Section 7.4 compares a
    published count against a count this study measures, the comparison rests on an
    assumption the sources do not license, and saying so is a fact about the sources rather
    than a criticism of them. The absence is now on record here rather than needing
    rediscovery.

  - The "~2.5M parameters, YOLOv10s" sentence appears in both papers and disagrees with
    both papers' own tables, which give YOLOv10s 2.6 and YOLOv11s 2.5. If the manuscript
    ever quotes a parameter count from the body text of either paper rather than from
    TABLE I, this is the sentence to avoid. Stated as a discrepancy requiring resolution:
    the table and the prose of each paper give different parameter counts for YOLOv10s, and
    this report takes the table as the value of record.
