FINDINGS

1. THE ABSOLUTE CLAIM HOLDS. Across all three papers there is no FPS figure, no
   milliseconds figure, no seconds-per-image or images-per-second figure, no latency value,
   no throughput value, and no inference-time value. Not in a table, not in body text, not
   on a figure axis, not in an abstract.

2. THE UNITS THAT WOULD CARRY SUCH A MEASUREMENT DO NOT OCCUR, with one spurious exception
   that is not a unit at all. Searching for FPS, "frames per second", "millisecond",
   "latency", "inference time", "throughput", "s/img", "img/s", "runtime", "run time" and
   "GPU time" returns ZERO hits in all three papers combined. The pattern \bms\b returns
   exactly ONE hit, and it fires on the letters "MS" in "MS COCO" -- the dataset name -- in
   [SAYEEDI] PDF p. 8. It is a case-insensitive token collision, not a milliseconds value.

IMPACT ON THE MANUSCRIPT

No change required. The absolute claim -- that none of the three papers reporting results on
ElectroCom61 reports a timing measurement of any kind -- is confirmed.

One sharpening is available, and it strengthens the claim rather than weakening it. All
three papers invoke real-time operation as a motivation: [ELHENIDY] and [GAIB] each assert
in their abstract that their model "achieves higher mAP and real-time inference response
than the previous YOLO versions", and both conclude that lightweight variants are "suitable
for low-power, real-time applications". Those are the strongest speed statements in the
corpus and none carries a number. If Section 6.4 or 7.4 wants to say why measuring latency
here is worth doing, the fact that the prior work asserts real-time suitability without ever
measuring it is the evidence for it, and it is stated as a fact about what is printed rather
than as a characterisation of the authors.

If the manuscript quotes a count from this check, quote 31 matching lines rather than 31
occurrences, and note that 12 of the 31 are reference-list titles rather than claims the
papers make in their own voice. The count of statements the three papers actually make about
speed is 18, and every one of them is unquantified.
