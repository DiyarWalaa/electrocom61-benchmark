3. THE MANUSCRIPT'S THREE CLAIMED ABSENCES ALL HOLD, in both papers:
     - No Ultralytics version. CONFIRMED. The only Ultralytics strings in either paper are
       two reference-list entries: [20] "Glenn Jocher and Ayush Chaurasia and Jing Qiu,
       Ultralytics YOLOv8, 2023. https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics" and [23]
       "Glenn Jocher, yolov5, 2020. Documentation at: https://docs.ultralytics.com/". Both
       are citations of other models, not a statement of the package version used to train.
     - No imgsz training argument. CONFIRMED. The string "imgsz" does not occur in either
       paper. A 640x640 figure IS printed in both, but as a preprocessing statement in
       Section II, not as a training argument -- see item 4 below.
     - No random seed. CONFIRMED. The word "seed" does not occur in either paper.

4. ONE ITEM NEEDS SPLITTING, because a blanket "not stated" would be wrong and a blanket
   "stated" would be misleading. Both papers print, in Section II "DATASET": "the photos
   were auto-oriented to standardise them and shrunk to 640x640 pixels, which included some
   stretching." That is an image-size statement about the dataset as prepared, and it is
   the same sentence Sayeedi's preprocessing section supports. It is NOT a statement of the
   imgsz argument passed at training time, and neither paper gives one.

5. TWO ITEMS GAIB STATES THAT ELHENIDY DOES NOT, and that the manuscript's list does not
   mention:
     - 3-fold cross-validation, k = 3, three validation runs, results averaged. Section III
       "Methodology", PDF pp. 2-3. Transcribed in full in C07.
     - Nothing else: the split ratio and class count below are stated by both.

6. TWELVE OF THE NINETEEN ITEMS ARE NOT STATED BY EITHER PAPER: momentum, PyTorch aside
   there is no CUDA version, no Python version, no seed, no patience or early stopping, no
   pretrained weights or transfer-learning initialisation, no Ultralytics version, no imgsz
   argument, no dataset version or Roboflow export version, no Mendeley version.

COULD NOT DETERMINE

Which specific P100 SKU either paper used. "The P100 Kaggle GPU" identifies the platform but
not the card variant, and no memory figure or driver version is printed.

Whether the 640x640 preprocessing figure was also the training imgsz. Both papers state the
first and neither states the second; treating them as the same value is an assumption the
sources do not license.

Whether the augmentations listed in Section II were applied by the training pipeline or
baked into the dataset beforehand. Both papers list the transforms without probabilities,
magnitudes or an application point.

IMPACT ON THE MANUSCRIPT

One correction is required and two refinements are available.

CORRECTION. The hardware should not be cited as "Tesla P100". Both papers print "The P100
Kaggle GPU is used for the training." The word Tesla appears in neither. If
data/config_provenance.csv or Section 2.4 carries "Tesla P100" attributed to these sources,
change it to "P100 Kaggle GPU" or quote the phrase. The card is almost certainly a Tesla
P100 -- that is what Kaggle provisions -- but the manuscript's own rule is that every number
and every attributed fact traces to what the source prints, and this one does not.

REFINEMENT 1. "lr0 0.01" attributes an Ultralytics argument name to papers that print only
"learning rate (0.01)". The value is right; the name is the manuscript's, not theirs. Same
for "PyTorch", which both papers spell "Pytorch". Neither is worth a hedge in the prose, but
if the configuration table quotes the sources it should quote what they wrote.

REFINEMENT 2. The "Sec. IV" citation is correct for the optimiser block -- epochs, batch,
learning rate, optimizer, weight decay, GPU, PyTorch -- and correct in both papers. It is
NOT the right section for four other items the manuscript may also be citing: the 640x640
resolution, the 70/20/10 split, the 61-class count and the augmentation list are all in
Section II "DATASET" in both papers, and Gaib's 3-fold cross-validation is in Section III
"Methodology". If a single citation covers all of these, split it.

Finally, a fact worth adding rather than a correction: Gaib states a 3-fold cross-validation
with k = 3 that Elhenidy does not, while dropping every variance column Elhenidy printed.
C07 covers the statistical consequences; for the configuration table the relevant point is
simply that the two papers' evaluation protocols are stated differently even though their
optimiser settings are stated identically, word for word.
